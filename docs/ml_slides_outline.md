# Data and ML Presentation Outline

Eight-slide outline for Member A's data and machine-learning section. Metrics below describe held-out synthetic episodes; they are not real-world performance claims.

## Slide 1 — South Lhonak case study and project scope

- GLOFs can release lake water downstream after moraine or ice-dam failure.
- South Lhonak Lake, Sikkim, and its October 2023 outburst are the case study.
- This repository is a prototype using synthetic sensor episodes.
- The included lake-area series is approximate published data, not this project's NDWI extraction.

**Show:** `data/lake_area_south_lhonak.csv` as a small table of the published observations; point out the pre-event and post-outburst rows.

**Speaker note:** “We are demonstrating a prototype workflow for the South Lhonak case study, not a field warning service.” “The lake-area values are cited approximations, while the sensor data and model evaluation are synthetic.”

## Slide 2 — Synthetic simulator and label design

- `ml/simulator.py` generates `normal` and `glof` episodes; GLOF types are rain, seepage, and seismic.
- The eight sensor fields are water level, seepage, dam tilt, inflow, seismic activity, rainfall, water temperature, and lake area.
- Normal episodes include sparse missing values and sensor spikes; these are simulated fault examples.
- Labels mark MEDIUM below 48 hours, HIGH below 24 hours, and CRITICAL below 12 hours before the simulated event.

**Show:** The event-type rows in `ml/results/per_event_type_recall.csv` to introduce the three simulated GLOF types.

**Speaker note:** “The labels and precursor patterns are generated by code, rather than collected from observed GLOFs.” “In seismic episodes the gradual precursor is weak, and the stronger trigger starts about 10 hours before the simulated event.”

## Slide 3 — Features, model, and episode-wise split

- The model uses 11 features: eight sensor readings and three six-hour changes (`water_level_d6`, `seepage_d6`, `tilt_d6`).
- `SimpleImputer(strategy="median")` learns fill values from training rows.
- The implemented classifier is `GradientBoostingClassifier`.
- The stratified, episode-wise 80/20 split uses 240 training and 60 held-out episodes from 300 generated episodes.

**Show:** `ml/results/feature_importance.png`.

**Speaker note:** “All rows from a given episode stay in one split, so correlated hours from one simulated episode do not appear in both training and testing.” “The chart ranks dam tilt as the largest plotted feature importance, with seismic activity next.”

## Slide 4 — Held-out model results

- LOW: precision 0.977, recall 0.994.
- MEDIUM: precision 0.836, recall 0.593; HIGH: 0.805 / 0.836.
- CRITICAL: precision 0.953, recall 0.950.
- Macro precision / recall / F1: 0.892 / 0.843 / 0.863.

**Show:** `ml/results/confusion_matrix.png` alongside the per-class precision/recall values from `ml/results/metrics.json`.

**Speaker note:** “These are results on held-out rows from the same synthetic data-generation process.” “The confusion matrix shows that the most prominent cross-class error is true MEDIUM rows classified as LOW.”

## Slide 5 — Event types, warning timing, and false alarms

- MEDIUM recall by event type is rain 0.921, seepage 0.968, and seismic 0.066.
- All 30 held-out GLOF episodes have a first model warning before their simulated event; none are unwarned.
- Across the warning-timing rows, mean delay is -7.366666666666666 hours and the worst late delay is 26 hours; negative delay means an earlier-than-label warning.
- On held-out normal data, 21 of 7,200 rows are predicted above LOW (fraction 0.002916666666666667).

**Show:** `ml/results/per_event_type_recall.csv` and a compact summary of `ml/results/warning_timing.csv` and `ml/results/false_alarms.json`.

**Speaker note:** “The overall score hides the simulator's weak seismic MEDIUM signal, so event-type recall matters.” “The timing and false-alarm values are synthetic-data diagnostics, not field-warning rates.”

## Slide 6 — Alert rule and persistence trade-off

- The dashboard takes the minimum predicted level over the current and preceding available row when `PERSISTENCE_HOURS = 2`.
- It alerts when the effective level rises above the remembered alert level, lowers the remembered level when risk falls, and repeats HIGH or CRITICAL after 12 hours.
- At k = 1, the held-out normal episodes produce 21 false alerts; at k = 2, 3, and 4, they produce 0.
- At k = 2, 30/30 GLOF episodes are alerted; first-alert median/minimum lead is 46/20 hours, and first-HIGH median/minimum is 22/18 hours.

**Show:** This table transcribed from `ml/results/alert_persistence.json`:

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 21 | 30/30 | 50 / 21 | 24 / 20 |
| 2 | 0 | 30/30 | 46 / 20 | 22 / 18 |
| 3 | 0 | 30/30 | 43.5 / 17 | 21 / 12 |

**Speaker note:** “The dashboard uses k = 2, which removed the normal-episode false alerts seen at k = 1 in this held-out synthetic study.” “That choice trades some lead time for fewer false alerts and is not a validated operational setting.”

## Slide 7 — Lake-area values and provenance

- `data/lake_area_south_lhonak.csv` contains five approximate published observations.
- Three rows cite the NRSC/ISRO map and are radar-based; two rows are secondary reports of Planet Labs optical analysis.
- The values were not computed by this project from Sentinel-2 NDWI.
- `ml/lake_area.py` loads this CSV for the dashboard's Lake Growth page.

**Show:** The five-row table in `data/lake_area_south_lhonak.csv`, including date, area, sensor/source, and note.

**Speaker note:** “The NRSC radar values are not directly comparable to an optical NDWI series, and the Planet Labs values are reported through secondary sources.” “The Earth Engine extraction script remains an untested skeleton, so none of its outputs appear in this table.”

## Slide 8 — Limitations and next work

- The model learns simulator patterns; held-out synthetic metrics may be optimistic and do not validate real sensor performance.
- Seismic MEDIUM recall is 0.066; the earlier seismic precursor is weak, and the stronger trigger starts about 10 hours before the event.
- Seismic warnings were late by 12–26 hours in 9 of 12 held-out seismic episodes; single-row blips motivate persistence filtering.
- Next: validate with real sensor records and historical events, implement Earth Engine NDWI extraction, improve seismic precursors, and calibrate probabilities.

**Show:** `ml/results/warning_timing.csv`, highlighting the seismic rows, with the event-type recall table from Slide 5 if space permits.

**Speaker note:** “There is no real sensor or historical-event validation in this repository, and this is not an operational warning system.” “The next steps require suitable independent data before any performance or probability claim can be made.”

## Likely questions and honest answers

**Why use synthetic data?**

The simulator is the available source of repeatable labeled episodes in this repository. It supports pipeline development, but results on its own generated patterns do not establish real-world performance.

**Why Gradient Boosting?**

It is the implemented classifier in `ml/train.py`. The repository does not include a model-comparison result showing that it is better than alternatives.

**Why is seismic MEDIUM recall so low?**

It is 0.066 in `per_event_type_recall.csv`. The simulator's gradual precursor is weak, while the stronger seismic trigger starts about 10 hours before the event.

**How many false alerts are there?**

The unfiltered model predicts 21 of 7,200 held-out normal rows above LOW. In the alert persistence study, k = 1 yields 21 normal alert events, while the dashboard's k = 2 yields 0 on the held-out normal episodes.

**Why use two-hour persistence?**

For these held-out synthetic episodes, k = 2 removes the false alerts observed at k = 1. Median first-alert lead changes from 50 hours to 46 hours, so this is a trade-off, not a validated operational setting.

**How would you validate the model on real data?**

It is not validated on real data. Future work needs time-stamped sensor records and confirmed historical event times, followed by an evaluation on data not used for fitting.

**Which sensor data does it need?**

The eight inputs are water level, seepage rate, dam tilt, inflow, seismic activity, rainfall, water temperature, and lake area; the reading also has `lake_id` and `timestamp`.

**What happens if a sensor fails or a reading is missing?**

The training pipeline fits median imputation on training rows, and the real predictor uses the saved imputer for missing sensor values. The repository does not implement sensor-failure detection.

**How is warning lead time computed?**

In the offline alert study it is simulated event hour minus first alert hour. The API's `estimated_lead_time_hrs` is a separate fixed mapping by risk level (LOW `None`, MEDIUM 36, HIGH 18, CRITICAL 6 hours), not a learned prediction.

**Why are lake-area values not from your own NDWI analysis?**

The current CSV contains five approximate published values. `data/extract_lake_area.py` is an untested Earth Engine skeleton with its bounding box and threshold marked for confirmation.

**Can this be deployed now?**

No. It has no real sensor validation, the input stream is not an operational field feed, the optional Telegram path has not had a live delivery test, and the project explicitly treats this as a prototype rather than an operational warning system.

**Are the risk probabilities calibrated?**

The score is a probability-weighted class-level score from 0 to 1, but the repository has no separate probability-calibration step. Calibration is listed as future work.
